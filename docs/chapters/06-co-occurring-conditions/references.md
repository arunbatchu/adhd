# References: Co-occurring Conditions

1. [Comorbidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comorbidity) - Wikipedia - The general concept of co-occurring conditions and why they complicate diagnosis and treatment — this chapter's organizing idea.

2. [Learning disability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_disability) - Wikipedia - Definitions and types of specific learning disabilities including dyslexia, ADHD's most common academic traveler.

3. [Bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder) - Wikipedia - The episodic mood disorder whose surface overlap with ADHD makes the chronic-versus-episodic distinction in this chapter matter.

4. Taking Charge of Adult ADHD (Second Edition) - Russell A. Barkley - Guilford Press - Barkley's comorbidity chapters established the practical habit this chapter teaches: re-screen before re-dosing, because a majority of ADHD arrives with company and treatment stalls usually mean something else is true.

5. Smart but Scattered - Peg Dawson and Richard Guare - Guilford Press - Dawson and Guare's executive-skills profile approach — mapping each child's specific strong and weak skills rather than treating diagnosis as destiny — shaped how clinicians untangle ADHD from look-alike and traveling conditions.

6. [ADHD Resource Center](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Resource_Centers/ADHD_Resource_Center/Home.aspx) - American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry - Professional-body guidance on ADHD with co-occurring anxiety, depression, ODD, and learning problems.

7. [ADHD and Sleep](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/mental-health/adhd-and-sleep) - Sleep Foundation - Clinician-reviewed coverage of delayed sleep phase, sleep apnea mimicry, and the amplifier loop this chapter describes.

8. [988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline](https://988lifeline.org/) - SAMHSA - The US crisis line referenced in this chapter's safety rules: call or text 988; available 24/7.

9. [ADHD - NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-adhd) - National Institute of Mental Health - Includes co-occurring condition overviews and links to NIMH pages for anxiety, depression, and autism.

10. [ADHD - MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/attentiondeficithyperactivitydisorder.html) - US National Library of Medicine - Gateway to vetted information on each traveling condition, useful when one of this chapter's screening tells rings true.
