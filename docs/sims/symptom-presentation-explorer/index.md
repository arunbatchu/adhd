## Description

Every ADHD evaluation counts the same eighteen behaviors. This explorer puts
the actual decision procedure in your hands: toggle symptom chips in the two
DSM-5 columns, watch the live counters, and see the resulting presentation the
moment a column crosses the threshold — six symptoms for children, five from
age seventeen (the **Adult** checkbox moves the line). Hovering any chip names
the executive function underneath it, connecting the checklist back to
[Chapter 2](../../chapters/02-the-adhd-brain/index.md)'s machinery.

Three presets tell the chapter's stories: **Marcus, 9** trips the visible
column; **Elena, 15** meets full criteria while disturbing no one; and
**Devon 9 → 39** (click twice) unchecks the visible motor chips and checks the
internal ones — the presentation shift that makes "he sits still now" mean
nothing. The banner always carries the caution that counts alone never
diagnose: the duration, onset, settings, and impairment gates of
[Chapter 5](../../chapters/05-getting-a-diagnosis/index.md) still apply.

## Lesson Plan

1. **Operate** (3 min): Build a pattern that yields each of the three presentations.
2. **Presets** (4 min): Run Marcus, then Elena. Ask: which one gets an evaluation this year, and why isn't it about severity?
3. **Shift** (3 min): Run Devon twice. Discuss what "grew out of it" gets wrong.
4. **Threshold** (2 min): With five inattentive chips on, toggle the Adult checkbox. Discuss why the DSM lowers the bar at 17.
