## Description

[Chapter 2](../../chapters/02-the-adhd-brain/index.md) argues that "she knows
how to do this" was never the point — executive functions are the skills that
manage other skills, and they fail one component at a time. This explorer
gives each of the six components a card: click to expand it into its
definition, concrete examples at three ages, the judgment that failure usually
attracts ("lazy," "rude," "careless"), and the replacement question a family
should ask instead.

**Quiz mode** is the point of the exercise: an everyday scenario appears
("he agreed to take out the trash, walked past it four times, and swears he
still meant to") and you attribute it to the right component. The feedback
explains the attribution — and why the near-misses differ. That attribution
move is exactly what the chapter wants families doing at home instead of
reaching for character verdicts.

## Lesson Plan

1. **Explore** (4 min): Open all six cards. Each learner names the component that hits hardest in their own household.
2. **Quiz** (5 min): Run all ten scenarios. Note which attributions were hardest.
3. **Apply** (5 min): Each learner writes one real scenario from their week and the group attributes it using the cards.
4. **Debrief** (2 min): Compare the "judged as" line with the "ask instead" line for the component your household picked.
