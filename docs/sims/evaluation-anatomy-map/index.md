## Description

[Chapter 5](../../chapters/05-getting-a-diagnosis/index.md) teaches that any
legitimate ADHD evaluation is built from the same components. This map lays
them out around the diagnostic conclusion: the clinical interview (the spine),
rating scales, self-report, collateral information, childhood history, and —
drawn dashed because they're optional or single data points — neuropsych
testing and computer attention tests. Two diamond gates carry the criteria:
onset before 12, and real impairment in two or more settings (with its known
blind spot: high-achievement masking). Click anything for what it contributes,
its limits, and the red flag of its absence.

**Rate-this-evaluation mode** is the payoff: four vignette evaluations — from
the 20-minute prescription mill to the solid two-session workup — that you
judge thorough or insufficient, with feedback naming the exact components
present or missing. That judgment is the consumer skill the chapter exists to
build.

## Lesson Plan

1. **Tour** (4 min): Click all seven components and both gates. Note which two are dashed and why.
2. **Rate** (5 min): Judge all four vignettes. Discuss the telehealth one — what makes it insufficient isn't the video call.
3. **Apply** (3 min): Draft the two questions you'd ask a prospective evaluator, using the map as the checklist.
