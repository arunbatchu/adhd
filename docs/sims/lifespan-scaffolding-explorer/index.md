## Description

[Chapter 4](../../chapters/04-adhd-across-the-lifespan/index.md)'s most useful
pattern in one picture: across seven life stages, executive demands rise while
external scaffolding drops — and the shaded gap between the lines is what a
person must supply themselves. Click any stage name for its typical ADHD
picture, what scaffolding just dropped, and the classic failure mode. The
college cliff is unmistakable: demands near their maximum exactly where
scaffolding hits its minimum.

Two controls carry the chapter's argument. The **rebuild toggle** overlays a
third line — support deliberately rebuilt through planners, coaching,
accommodations, and family agreements — visibly narrowing the gap (that line
is what Part Three of the book builds). And **Follow Amara** walks the
chapter's undiagnosed case across the stages: coping, coping, straining,
collapse at college — then asks where an observer would first notice anything,
versus where the condition was present.

## Lesson Plan

1. **Read the shape** (3 min): Identify the widest gap before toggling anything. Ask why crises cluster there.
2. **Stage stories** (4 min): Click each stage; have learners match a person they know to a stage.
3. **Amara** (3 min): Step through the walkthrough. Discuss why "she was fine until college" misreads the data.
4. **Rebuild** (3 min): Toggle the third line. Ask which specific supports would move *your* stage's green dot up.
