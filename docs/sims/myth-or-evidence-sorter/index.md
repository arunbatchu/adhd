## Description

The sorter deals claims about ADHD one at a time — some drawn straight from
[Chapter 1](../../chapters/01-what-adhd-is/index.md)'s myth section, some from
the research facts around it. You must commit to a judgment (*Myth* or
*Supported by evidence*) before the card flips; the reveal always comes with a
two-to-three sentence explanation naming the concept the answer rests on —
attention regulation, heritability, the DSM-5 counting rules, masking.

A round is ten claims. The summary lists anything you missed, with its
explanation, so a replay targets exactly the beliefs that need updating. The
prediction-before-reveal design is deliberate: judging first is what makes
this an evaluation exercise instead of a reading exercise.

## Lesson Plan

1. **Baseline** (5 min): Each learner plays one solo round and notes their score.
2. **Pair round** (5 min): Pairs replay, debating each claim aloud before committing. Scores usually rise — discuss why.
3. **Family variant** (5 min): The person with ADHD and a family member play together; each privately predicts which claims the *other* will miss.
4. **Debrief** (3 min): Collect the most-missed claims and trace each to its chapter section.
