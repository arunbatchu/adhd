## Description

The three trait words behind every ADHD diagnosis — inattention,
hyperactivity, impulsivity — never look the same at six, sixteen, and
thirty-six. This explorer lays the nine combinations out as a grid. Click any
cell to see concrete example behaviors, a first-person quote from inside that
experience, and the misreading that presentation usually attracts.

The **"Who gets noticed?"** toggle re-shades the grid by how likely each
presentation is to trigger a referral for evaluation. The pale cells — quiet
inattention, adult restlessness turned inward — are where ADHD hides, and
they explain most of the late-diagnosis stories in
[Chapter 4](../../chapters/04-adhd-across-the-lifespan/index.md).

## Lesson Plan

1. **Orient** (2 min): Ask learners which single cell matches their mental image of "ADHD." (Most pick Hyperactivity/Child.)
2. **Explore** (5 min): Click through all nine cells. Have each learner find the cell that best matches someone they know.
3. **Reveal** (3 min): Turn on the "Who gets noticed?" toggle. Ask: which cells are dark, which are pale, and who do the pale cells describe?
4. **Connect** (3 min): Discuss why girls and adults are diagnosed late using only the grid's shading as evidence.
