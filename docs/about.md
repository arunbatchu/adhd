# About This Book

Someone gets an ADHD diagnosis. It might be at eight years old, or at
twenty-four, or at fifty. Whenever it lands, it lands on more than one person.
The person diagnosed has a hundred questions and a lot of history to reread.
The people around them — a parent, a partner, a sibling, a friend — have their
own hundred questions, and usually nowhere good to take them.

Most ADHD books pick one of those readers. This one is written for both at
once.

## Who This Book Is For

**If you have ADHD**, this book explains the machinery you're running on, what
the evidence actually supports, and how to build systems that work with your
brain instead of against it. It never treats you as a problem to be managed.

**If you love someone with ADHD**, this book explains what they're up against,
what genuinely helps, and — just as important — what backfires. It takes your
strain seriously too, instead of treating you as either a saint or a nuisance.

Both readers are competent adults doing something hard. The book is written
that way throughout.

You need no medical or psychology background. Every clinical term is explained
in plain language the first time it appears. The reading level is roughly
ninth to tenth grade.

## What's Different About It

**Every major topic addresses both sides.** Not a caregiver chapter bolted onto
a book for patients — both perspectives run through all fifteen chapters. When
Chapter 14 covers reminders, it covers how to give one and how to receive one.

**It takes late diagnosis seriously.** Plenty of people reach adulthood before
anyone names what's going on, especially women, whose ADHD is often hidden
behind masking and good grades. Chapter 4 is largely about them, and Chapter 11
deals with the grief and relief that follow a late diagnosis.

**It respects adult autonomy.** Supporting an adult family member is not
parenting them. That distinction is a first-class concern here, not an
afterthought — see Chapters 14 and 15.

**It cares for the caregiver.** Strain, burnout, and boundaries get real
coverage. Support that destroys the supporter helps nobody.

**It's honest in both directions.** ADHD is a genuine disability where it is
one, and a genuine difference with real strengths. This book romanticizes
neither.

## How to Read It

Chapters run in dependency order, so reading start to finish always works. But
most people arrive with an urgent question, so the book is also built for
jumping in:

- **Just diagnosed?** Chapters 1, 2, and 11.
- **Deciding about medication?** Chapters 6 and 7.
- **Pursuing a diagnosis?** Chapters 4 and 5.
- **Struggling with daily systems?** Chapters 9 and 10.
- **Fighting at home?** Chapters 14 and 15.
- **A parent or partner who wants the short version?** Read Chapter 14 first,
  then come back to the beginning.

The best way to use it: read the same chapter as the person you're reading it
with, then compare what rang true. Several chapters end with prompts designed
for exactly that conversation.

The book ends with two documents you write yourselves — a personal owner's
manual, written by the person with ADHD, and a family support plan, written by
the people around them. Chapter 15 walks through both.

## A Note on Limits

This book explains; it does not diagnose or prescribe. It covers what an
evaluation involves, but it cannot replace one. It explains how ADHD
medications work, but dosing decisions belong to you and your prescriber.
Where evidence is thin or contested, the book says so instead of picking a
side and sounding confident.

If you are in crisis, please contact a clinician or, in the US, call or text
988 for the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.

## Why It Was Written

This book was written for families in the months after a new ADHD diagnosis —
the period when the questions come faster than the answers, and when the
people who want to help have the least idea how. The material that exists is
mostly split: clinical for professionals, tips for adults with ADHD, or advice
for parents of young children. Very little is written for an adult with ADHD
and the people who love them, to read together.

That gap is what this book is for.
