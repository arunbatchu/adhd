    Course Description¶

    Understanding ADHD: A Guidebook for People with ADHD and the People Who Care for Them¶

    This guidebook is a comprehensive, evidence-based resource on Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD). It combines the science of ADHD with practical, day-to-day strategies, and it is written for two readers at once: the person who has ADHD, and the people who love and support them — parents, spouses, partners, siblings, and close friends. A new diagnosis lands on a whole family, not just one person. This book is for the whole family.

    Target Audience¶

    This textbook is designed for:

    • Adults and teens who have been diagnosed with ADHD, or who suspect they have it — especially adults diagnosed late, and women whose ADHD went unrecognized for years
    • Parents of a child, teen, or adult child with ADHD — including parents learning how to support a grown daughter or son without parenting them like a child
    • Spouses and partners of someone with ADHD, now or in the future
    • Siblings, close friends, and extended family who want to understand and help
    • Teachers, coaches, and mentors without specialized ADHD training
    • General learners interested in understanding ADHD and neurodiversity

    We assume our reader has no medical or psychological background. Every clinical term is explained in plain language when it first appears.

    Reading Level¶

    Written at a 9th-10th grade reading level for maximum accessibility, with complex concepts explained using clear language, diagrams, interactive simulations, and real-world examples.

    Prerequisites¶

    None. All essential concepts are explained from the ground up.

    How to Use This Guidebook¶

    • Sequential reading: Work through chapters in order for a complete understanding
    • Reference guide: Jump to specific topics as questions come up — before a doctor's visit, a school meeting, or a hard conversation
    • Shared reading: The person with ADHD and a caregiver read the same chapter and compare what rang true — the book is built for that conversation
    • Educational course: Follow the structured learning path with quizzes, checklists, and activities

    Learning Objectives¶

    By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

    Remember (Knowledge):

    • Define key terms including ADHD, executive function, working memory, dopamine, hyperactivity, inattention, impulsivity, and emotional dysregulation
    • List the three presentations of ADHD (inattentive, hyperactive-impulsive, combined) and their distinguishing features
    • Identify the common signs of ADHD at each life stage: childhood, adolescence, and adulthood
    • Recall the major evidence-based treatment options and the professionals who provide them
    • Name the most common co-occurring conditions (anxiety, depression, learning disabilities, sleep disorders)

    Understand (Comprehension):

    • Explain how ADHD affects brain development, attention regulation, and the brain's reward system
    • Describe why ADHD is a difference in regulation, not a deficit of intelligence, effort, or character
    • Explain why ADHD often looks different in girls and women, and why they are diagnosed later
    • Interpret what happens in a diagnostic evaluation and what a diagnosis does and does not mean
    • Understand how stimulant and non-stimulant medications work, and what they can and cannot do
    • Describe rejection sensitivity, time blindness, and hyperfocus, and why they occur

    Apply (Application):

    • Use evidence-based strategies for daily structure: routines, reminders, body doubling, and externalizing memory
    • Apply communication techniques that reduce conflict between a person with ADHD and their family
    • Implement school and workplace accommodations, and navigate the process of requesting them
    • Use tools and technology (planners, timers, apps) matched to how ADHD brains actually work
    • Practice self-advocacy: explaining one's own ADHD to teachers, employers, friends, and partners

    Analyze (Analysis):

    • Compare treatment options — medication, behavioral therapy, coaching, exercise, sleep, nutrition — and the evidence behind each
    • Distinguish ADHD from look-alike conditions and identify when co-occurring conditions need their own attention
    • Analyze recurring family conflicts (chores, homework, lateness, forgotten promises) to find the executive-function breakdown underneath the behavior
    • Break down a failing routine or system to find where it fights the ADHD brain instead of working with it
    • Examine how ADHD shows up differently across life stages and relationships — child and parent, student and teacher, partner and partner

    Evaluate (Evaluation):

    • Assess whether a treatment plan is working, using concrete criteria and tracking
    • Judge the quality of ADHD information and products — separating evidence from myth, marketing, and social-media misinformation
    • Evaluate when to seek a professional: diagnosis, medication review, therapy, coaching, or crisis support
    • Assess caregiver strain and recognize when the supporter needs support
    • Critique one's own strategies honestly: keep what works, drop what doesn't, without shame

    Create (Synthesis):

    • Develop a personalized support plan that fits the individual's ADHD profile, strengths, and goals
    • Design home routines and physical environments that reduce friction for an ADHD brain
    • Create a family communication agreement: how to give reminders, handle slip-ups, and repair after conflict
    • Build a care team and support network — clinicians, coaches, school or workplace allies, peer community
    • Compose a personal "owner's manual": how my ADHD works, what helps, what hurts, and what I need from the people around me

    Topics Covered¶

    This guidebook is organized into these major areas:

    1. What ADHD Is — and Is Not: definitions, history, myths, and the neurodiversity perspective
    2. The ADHD Brain: brain development, executive function, dopamine, and the science of attention
    3. The Three Presentations: inattentive, hyperactive-impulsive, and combined
    4. ADHD Across the Lifespan: childhood, adolescence, college years, adulthood, and diagnosis at any age
    5. ADHD in Girls and Women: masking, high achievement hiding the struggle, late diagnosis, hormonal influences
    6. Getting a Diagnosis: evaluation, rating scales, who diagnoses, and what a diagnosis means
    7. Co-occurring Conditions: anxiety, depression, learning disabilities, sleep problems, and more
    8. Medication: stimulants, non-stimulants, how they work, side effects, and finding the right fit
    9. Non-medication Treatment: behavioral therapy, ADHD coaching, exercise, sleep, and nutrition
    10. Executive Function Skills: time, memory, organization, planning, and getting started
    11. Emotional Life with ADHD: emotional dysregulation, rejection sensitivity, shame, self-esteem, and making sense of your own history after a late diagnosis
    12. Strengths and Thriving: creativity, energy, hyperfocus, and building a life that fits
    13. School and Learning: study strategies, accommodations, IEPs and 504 plans, college supports
    14. Work and Career: choosing work that fits, workplace accommodations, disclosure decisions
    15. Family Life: supporting a family member with ADHD at any age — from parenting a child to standing beside an adult — chores and conflict, communication that works, and respecting an adult's autonomy while still helping
    16. Partners and Relationships: dating, marriage, and the caregiver-partner dynamic
    17. Caring for the Caregiver: strain, burnout, and support for the supporters
    18. Daily Systems and Tools: routines, environment design, planners, timers, and technology
    19. Self-Advocacy and Identity: owning the diagnosis, explaining it to others, community
    20. The Road Ahead: current research, emerging treatments, and living well long-term

    Topics Not Covered¶

    To keep the book focused and safe, this guidebook does not cover:

    • Medical dosing advice or medication selection for a specific person — that belongs to the prescribing clinician
    • Diagnosis of any individual — the book explains the process but cannot replace an evaluation
    • Autism spectrum disorder in depth (it is discussed only where it co-occurs with ADHD)
    • Legal advice on disability law beyond a general orientation to rights and accommodations
    • Alternative treatments without a credible evidence base, except to explain how to evaluate such claims

    Scope and Depth¶

    This guidebook provides:

    • Evidence-based coverage of ADHD science, diagnosis, and treatment, drawn from current research and consensus guidelines
    • Practical, actionable strategies for daily life, school, work, and relationships
    • Both sides of every topic: what the person with ADHD experiences, and what the caregiver can do
    • Real-world scenarios showing strategies in action
    • Interactive elements including checklists, self-assessment tools, conversation guides, tracking templates, and interactive simulations (MicroSims) of core concepts

    What Makes This Resource Unique¶

    • Dual audience by design: every major topic addresses the person with ADHD and their caregivers together, so both sides read the same book and meet in the middle
    • Family-centered: written from the conviction that ADHD outcomes depend on the alliance around the person, not just the person
    • Strengths-honest: takes ADHD seriously as a disability where it is one, and seriously as a difference with real strengths — without romanticizing either
    • Non-clinical perspective: written for families, not professionals, with every term explained
    • Lifespan view: follows ADHD from childhood through adulthood, including the transitions — high school to college, college to work, dating to partnership — where support systems change hands
    • Interactive learning: combines reading with simulations, quizzes, and practical activities
