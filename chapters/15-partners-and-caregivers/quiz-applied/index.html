    Applied Quiz: Partners And Caring For The Caregiver¶

    The final set of questions, weighted toward applying the ideas rather than recalling them. Several ask you to work the two capstone documents.

    1. Six months in, a partner feels the intense early attentiveness fading and wonders whether they are being fallen out of love with. What is actually happening?¶

    1. The novelty curve doing what it does — what interest powers, habituation taxes, so the attention that felt effortless now requires deliberate systems
    2. A genuine decline in feelings that should be addressed directly
    3. Early-relationship attentiveness was performed rather than real
    4. The relationship has entered a phase that predicts poor long-term outcomes
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Early dating plays to this brain's strengths — novelty, intensity, an endlessly interesting new person — which is why partners often describe the early months as the most attended-to they've ever felt. The honest warning lives in the same fact. The chapter's remedy is that lasting couples convert early-romance behaviors into built structures before the fade: planned dates onto the shared calendar, long conversations into protected weekly time.

    2. When should someone tell a new romantic partner about their ADHD?¶

    1. On the first date, so the partner can make an informed choice
    2. Early in seriousness — after patterns are visible but before the first fight about them
    3. Only if the relationship becomes long-term
    4. Immediately after the first ADHD-related conflict, when it will be most understandable
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Unlike an employer, a serious partner will eventually live inside your ADHD, so the question is never whether but when and how. The first date offers context-free information that invites stereotype; after the first fight, disclosure as excuse arrives pre-discredited — the same timing trap as Chapter 13. What the disclosure buys is the most valuable currency exchange in an ADHD relationship: it converts future incidents from evidence about caring into known machinery with known workarounds.

    See: Chapter 15

    3. In a marriage, one partner has become the tracker, reminder, and checker, and the other feels supervised. Why does the research treat this as so serious?¶

    1. It creates an unfair distribution of household work
    2. It indicates that one partner is controlling by nature
    3. Nobody sustains desire for their case manager, and nobody desires someone who treats them like a case
    4. It usually signals that the ADHD is inadequately treated
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The research on ADHD-affected couples names this dynamic as the strongest predictor of the misery that follows, and the chapter gives the blunt reason. The exits are structural: the load moves to systems rather than to the non-ADHD partner — the calendar does the reminding, not the wife — the fitted division replaces the drifted one, and the couple re-books the roles the drift crowded out. Option B is exactly the character reading the chapter rejects, since the trap is a systemic drift.

    Concept Tested: Partner-Parent Trap

    See: Chapter 15

    4. Chores are split evenly, yet one partner is exhausted from remembering the dentist, the detergent, the birthdays, and the permission slips. What is the fix?¶

    1. Ask the other partner to notice more actively
    2. Rebalance the visible chores further in the tired partner's favor
    3. Alternate who holds the tracking each month
    4. Make the invisible inventory visible and system-borne — one shared list and calendar, recurring items automated, and whole domains transferred with their tracking included
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Mental load is almost pure prospective memory plus planning — Chapter 2's two least reliable functions — which produces the standard asymmetry where the noticing concentrates in the non-ADHD partner. The chapter is explicit that the fix is not "notice more," since Chapter 1 retired that genre. It closes with the key distinction: handing someone a task while keeping its tracking hands them an errand, not a share of the load.

    See: Chapter 15

    5. A couple tries generic counseling and finds the communication exercises unhelpful. What should they look for instead, and when?¶

    1. ADHD-informed counseling — ask directly about experience with ADHD couples — when the trap has hardened, the ledgers are long, or the same fight has run monthly for a year
    2. Individual therapy for each partner separately
    3. A different generic counselor, since technique varies by practitioner
    4. Counseling only after the ADHD is fully medicated and stable
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Generic tools assume two neurotypical nervous systems and underperform exactly the way generic CBT did in Chapter 8. ADHD-informed counseling treats the trap as a systemic drift rather than one partner's controllingness or the other's fecklessness, and — often its biggest single contribution — gives the non-ADHD partner room to say the resentments out loud in a container that keeps them from landing as character verdicts.

    6. A parent says "I'm the one who holds everything together" and irritably refuses offers of help. Where is she in the progression of strain?¶

    1. Early strain, which rest alone will resolve
    2. The dangerous stage, where strain has calcified into identity — the martyrdom is load-bearing, and help gets refused because being needed has become the compensation
    3. Ordinary caregiver tiredness, requiring no intervention
    4. Compassion fatigue, which is the collapse point itself
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Strain's progression runs tiredness, then resentment and the ledger, then identity. The chapter gives self-screening tells: you can't remember your last unscheduled afternoon, your inner monologue about your person is mostly audit, you've stopped doing the things that were yours, and offers of help irritate you — two or more means the section is speaking directly to you. Compassion fatigue belongs to burnout, the collapse point, which is a later stage than this.

    See: Chapter 15

    7. A supporter feels selfish scheduling her choir practice while her partner is struggling. How does the chapter answer?¶

    1. Her instinct is right; support should take priority during difficult periods
    2. She should attend only when things are calm at home
    3. Her own things belong on the calendar with the same standing as anyone's appointments, because what isn't scheduled gets eaten
    4. She should replace choir with something less time-consuming
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Caregiver self-care is defined by what it isn't — not spa clichés, but the boring load-bearing basics the book prescribed for the ADHD reader all along, applied without apology to the other one: protected sleep, own things calendared, own care team where needed, own honest tracking of the strain tells. The airline instruction is the section's thesis, and the chapter insists it is not a metaphor to be admired but a procedure to be followed: a supporter who goes down supports no one.

    8. A wife has been managing her adult husband's unopened mail and medication refills for years. What does the boundary work ask of her, and how does the chapter frame it?¶

    1. Continue managing them, since the consequences would otherwise fall on the household
    2. Stop abruptly so he experiences the full consequences at once
    3. Hand the tasks to another family member instead
    4. Let the systems and their owner carry it — "I love you, I'm not tracking this for you, and I trust your system — want help building one?" — since declining to manage is the job description correctly read, not abandonment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter says Chapter 14's autonomy rules protect the caregiver too: their unopened mail, their medication choices, and their consequences are not hers to manage. Note the shape of the boundary sentence — it declines the tracking while offering genuine help with building a system, which keeps it from being option B's abrupt withdrawal. Respite is the structural other half: genuinely off-duty time, not time off spent auditing from a distance.

    9. A family plans to write a document laying out how they will manage their adult son's appointments, medication, and deadlines. What is wrong with this plan?¶

    1. The Family Support Plan is a plan for the family's own conduct and sustainability — not a management plan for the person, which Chapter 14 forbade
    2. Nothing, provided the son agrees to the contents
    3. It should be written by a clinician rather than the family
    4. It should be combined with the Personal Owner's Manual into a single document
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The Family Support Plan's sections are all about the family: our understanding, our agreements, our lines (the autonomy commitments and the narrow safety exception, written down so nobody renegotiates mid-crisis), each supporter's role including the overlooked-sibling check, the caregiver's own plan, and a review date. Option D misses the design: they are two documents written by different people for each other, and exchanged.

    10. A family exchanges their two capstone documents and notices that nothing in either is new — every sentence came from the book. What is the point, then?¶

    1. The documents mainly serve as a record for future clinicians
    2. Repetition is the mechanism, and rewriting the content aids retention
    3. That it is written, owned, and exchanged — the family's working knowledge externalized, the way everything that matters to an ADHD household eventually must be
    4. The exercise reveals which chapters were misunderstood
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. In the closing example, Sana's manual makes her mother cry at the masking section and finally gives her father a job he can do right; the family's plan has its "our lines" section signed by the mother who sent eleven articles, and schedules the choir practice she'd quietly dropped three years ago. The chapter's last line on it is that this is the book doing what it taught — externalization, applied to the family's own knowledge. Both are drafts, dated, revised annually, because everything decays, documents included.
