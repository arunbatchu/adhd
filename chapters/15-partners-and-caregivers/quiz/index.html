    Quiz: Partners And Caring For The Caregiver¶

    Test your understanding of romantic partnership with ADHD, caregiver strain, and the book's two closing documents with these review questions.

    1. What is the "partner-parent trap," as described in this chapter?¶

    1. A legal requirement that partners must formally adopt caregiving duties
    2. A financial trap where couples overspend on ADHD treatments
    3. A parenting technique recommended for raising children with ADHD
    4. A dynamic where one partner slides into a logistics-manager role while the other is treated like a supervised teenager, eroding desire on both sides
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The partner-parent trap is the dynamic where one partner drifts into logistics-manager and the other into supervised teenager, which research on ADHD-affected couples names as the strongest predictor of relationship misery, since nobody sustains desire for their case manager. Option A invents a legal requirement that doesn't exist. Option B misplaces the trap as financial rather than relational. Option C confuses it with an unrelated parenting technique.

    Concept Tested: Partner-Parent Trap

    See: Chapter 15

    2. Why does this chapter recommend transferring whole domains — including their tracking — rather than just individual tasks when dividing mental load?¶

    1. Because tasks are illegal to divide individually under family law
    2. Because tracking is impossible to transfer between partners under any circumstances
    3. Because handing someone a task while keeping its tracking gives them an errand, not a real share of the invisible load that causes burnout
    4. Because domains are always easier to complete than individual tasks
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter is explicit that handing someone a task while keeping its tracking hands them an errand, not a share of the load — real division transfers whole domains, noticing included, such as "you own everything car-shaped." Option A invents a legal claim. Option B contradicts the chapter's entire recommendation, which assumes tracking can and should transfer. Option D makes an unsupported claim about task difficulty.

    See: Chapter 15

    3. According to this chapter, what is the dangerous final stage in caregiver strain's typical progression, and why is it especially hard to reverse?¶

    1. Identity calcification — "I'm the one who holds everything together" — because at that point being needed has become the caregiver's own compensation, so help gets refused
    2. Tiredness, because it is the mildest and most temporary stage
    3. Resentment, because it disappears completely once named out loud
    4. Burnout, because it is identical to ordinary tiredness with no distinguishing features
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter traces strain from tiredness through resentment to identity calcification, where being needed becomes the caregiver's own compensation, making help feel like a threat rather than relief. Option B misidentifies the mildest stage as the dangerous final one. Option C wrongly claims resentment resolves on its own. Option D denies the distinct signature the chapter gives caregiver burnout.

    Concept Tested: Caregiver Strain

    See: Chapter 15

    4. Someone in a two-month-old relationship is deciding when to tell their new partner about their ADHD. Based on this chapter's guidance, what is the best timing and approach?¶

    1. Disclose in detail on the very first date, before any patterns are visible
    2. Wait until after the first serious argument caused by an ADHD-related pattern, so there's a clear example to point to
    3. Disclose early in seriousness, once some patterns have become visible, using a warm mechanism-pattern-ask formula rather than either extreme
    4. Never disclose, since ADHD is a private matter with no bearing on a relationship
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter recommends disclosing early in seriousness, once patterns are visible enough to attach the explanation to, using the warm mechanism-pattern-ask formula — not on the first date and not after the first fight, when it reads as excuse. Option A shares context-free information too early. Option B repeats the same timing trap Chapter 13 warned about for employers. Option D contradicts the chapter's framing that a serious partner will eventually live inside the condition regardless.

    See: Chapter 15

    5. A mother realizes she hasn't had an unscheduled afternoon in over a year, thinks about her son mostly in terms of auditing his behavior, and feels irritated whenever someone offers to help. Using this chapter's self-screening tells, what should she recognize and do?¶

    1. These are caregiver strain's warning tells, and the recommended move is to protect her own scheduled respite time and treat her own self-care — sleep, her own activities, honest tracking of these tells — as seriously as any other appointment
    2. Nothing; these feelings are a normal and permanent part of caregiving that cannot be addressed
    3. She should hide these feelings from everyone, since admitting them would be a betrayal of her son
    4. She should immediately hand over all caregiving responsibilities to someone else and disengage entirely
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter names these exact signs — no unscheduled time, audit-mode thinking, irritation at offered help — as the strain tells it asks readers to self-screen for, with the remedy being scheduled respite and self-care treated with the same standing as any other appointment. Option B dismisses tells the chapter treats as actionable warnings. Option C encourages hiding feelings the chapter instead wants named. Option D overcorrects into total disengagement, which the chapter doesn't recommend.

    6. A couple negotiates a "squall protocol" for their relationship, agreeing in advance that either partner can take a short pause during a heated moment. One evening, the partner with ADHD says "I need ten minutes" and steps outside during an argument. Based on this chapter, what should happen next?¶

    1. The other partner should follow immediately and continue the argument to avoid losing momentum
    2. The pause should be used to end the relationship discussion permanently
    3. The partner taking the pause should be criticized afterward for walking away
    4. The pause should be honored as agreed, and the conversation should genuinely resume afterward rather than being treated as avoidance
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's squall protocol requires the pause to be pre-agreed and the return guaranteed — honoring the pause and genuinely resuming afterward is what makes the protocol trustworthy for both partners. Option A defeats the protocol's purpose by refusing to honor it. Option B misuses the pause as a way to avoid the topic permanently. Option C punishes the exact skill the chapter wants recognized as constructive.

    7. A couple attends generic marriage counseling that assumes both partners have typical neurotypical communication patterns, and it doesn't seem to help their recurring conflicts. Using this chapter's analysis, what is the most likely explanation?¶

    1. Counseling never works for any couple facing any kind of challenge
    2. The couple must not actually be trying hard enough in the sessions
    3. Generic counseling tools often underperform for ADHD-affected couples because they don't account for the mechanism-based dynamics — like the partner-parent trap — that drive the specific conflict pattern; ADHD-informed counseling addresses this directly
    4. Only medication, never counseling, can help an ADHD-affected relationship
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter is explicit that generic counseling underperforms for ADHD-affected couples the same way generic CBT underperformed in Chapter 8, and recommends seeking counselors who directly address ADHD-specific dynamics. Option A overgeneralizes a specific mismatch into a blanket claim. Option B blames effort rather than fit, which the chapter never does. Option D dismisses counseling entirely, contradicting the chapter's endorsement of ADHD-informed counseling.

    8. A family has been reading this book together, but a year later notices old fights have crept back and the systems they built have quietly decayed. Using this chapter's closing analysis, what does this pattern mean, and what is the recommended response?¶

    1. It means the systems ran their normal lifecycle into the maintenance stage — decay is normal operation, not proof anything was fake — and the move is to avoid character verdicts, rebuild smaller, and revise the capstone documents with a year of new data
    2. It means the whole approach failed and the relationship is fundamentally broken
    3. It means the person with ADHD never really wanted to change
    4. It means the family should give up on any further documents or systems permanently
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's final check-yourself question addresses this exact scenario: decay is normal operation, not relapse, and the move is the maintenance habit practiced throughout the book — no character verdicts, rebuild smaller, and revise both capstone documents with fresh data. Option B and C both convert an expected systems event into a verdict on the relationship or the person, which the chapter explicitly rejects. Option D contradicts the chapter's message that renewal, not abandonment, is the plan.

    9. A couple has had the same fight about household logistics every month for over a year, and the non-ADHD partner has become the default tracker of nearly every recurring task despite repeated conversations. Weighing this chapter's guidance on when to seek couples counseling, which judgment is best supported?¶

    1. Couples counseling is unnecessary as long as the couple still loves each other, regardless of how long a pattern has persisted
    2. This is exactly the kind of hardened, long-running pattern this chapter identifies as a clear signal to seek ADHD-informed couples counseling, rather than continuing to try the same conversations alone
    3. The couple should avoid counseling because it would be an admission that the relationship has already failed
    4. Any pattern lasting less than five years is too minor to justify seeking outside help
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. This scenario matches the chapter's stated signal for seeking counseling — a hardened pattern with a monthly fight running for a year — which describes the partner-parent trap needing outside, ADHD-informed help rather than more of the same unaided conversations. Option A dismisses a chronic pattern the chapter treats as a real warning sign. Option C frames help-seeking as failure, which the chapter directly rejects. Option D invents an arbitrary time threshold the chapter never states.

    10. A family wants to build a lasting structure that captures both what the person with ADHD needs from supporters and what the family commits to doing (and not doing) to sustain itself over time. Which combination best synthesizes this chapter's guidance into that structure?¶

    1. A single verbal conversation, held once, with no written record kept afterward
    2. A Personal Owner's Manual written by the person with ADHD paired with a Family Support Plan written by the family, both exchanged, dated, and revisited at a scheduled review date as systems inevitably decay
    3. A single document written entirely by the caregiver on behalf of the person with ADHD, describing what the family will manage for them
    4. Separate documents that are never shared with each other, kept private to avoid conflict
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's two capstones are designed exactly this way — one document from each side, exchanged, dated, and revisited at scheduled review points, since everything in the book eventually needs this kind of externalization. Option A skips the externalization the whole book has argued for. Option C recreates the management-not-support pattern the book warns against throughout. Option D removes the exchange that gives the documents their purpose as an alliance.
