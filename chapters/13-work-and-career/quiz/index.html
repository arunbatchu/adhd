    Quiz: Work And Career¶

    Test your understanding of career fit, workplace accommodations, disclosure, and burnout prevention with these review questions.

    1. What does "career fit" mean according to this chapter?¶

    1. Finding a job listed on a "best careers for ADHD" website
    2. Choosing the highest-paying job available regardless of daily tasks
    3. Working exclusively in creative fields, since ADHD only fits creative work
    4. A role where what the job pays for overlaps with what the person's brain naturally produces, while the "war column" of poor-fit tasks stays small or delegated
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Career fit means a role where the job's paid-for column overlaps with what the person's brain naturally produces, with the difficult "war column" tasks kept small or delegated, illustrated by the nurse who thrived in the ER but struggled in records and compliance. Option A is exactly the internet genre the chapter warns against. Option B ignores daily task fit entirely. Option C wrongly restricts fit to one field, which the chapter never claims.

    2. In plain language, what does the ADA require of employers regarding ADHD?¶

    1. Employers with 15 or more employees must provide reasonable accommodations to qualified employees whose ADHD substantially limits major life activities, through a good-faith interactive process
    2. Employers must automatically promote any employee who discloses ADHD
    3. Employers must fire any employee who cannot perform every task without accommodation
    4. Employers are legally forbidden from ever asking about accommodations
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter explains that ADA-covered employers must provide reasonable accommodations to qualified employees through a good-faith interactive process, while the employee must still perform the job's essential functions. Option B invents an unrelated promotion requirement. Option C contradicts the entire purpose of accommodations, which exist so employees don't need to be fired. Option D misstates the interactive process the ADA actually requires.

    3. What is "graduated disclosure," as described in this chapter?¶

    1. Announcing a diagnosis to the entire company on the first day of a new job
    2. Never telling anyone at work about an ADHD diagnosis under any circumstances
    3. Starting with informal, specific asks with no diagnosis attached, then escalating to partial or full formal disclosure only if needed
    4. A legal requirement to disclose ADHD within the first 90 days of employment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Graduated disclosure starts with informal, no-diagnosis-attached specific asks, escalating to partial and then formal disclosure only as needed, reserving the ADA's full machinery for when it's actually required. Option A is the opposite of graduated — it's maximal disclosure on day one. Option B forecloses a strategic option the chapter treats as legitimate but not mandatory. Option D invents a legal deadline that doesn't exist.

    4. Why does this chapter warn against "top 10 careers for ADHD" lists and describe ADHD-friendly work as a shape instead?¶

    1. Because no job is ever suitable for someone with ADHD
    2. Because such lists are always completely accurate and reliable
    3. Because only medical doctors can determine appropriate careers for people with ADHD
    4. Because fit depends on structural features like urgency, novelty, visible results, and movement, which can appear in many different jobs and professions, not a fixed list of job titles
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter calls career-list genres "astrology with a salary column" because what actually matters is a shape — urgency, novelty, visible results, movement — that shows up across many different jobs, not a fixed set of titles. Option A contradicts the chapter's entire premise that good fits exist. Option B is the opposite of the chapter's warning. Option C invents an unsupported credentialing requirement.

    Concept Tested: ADHD-Friendly Jobs

    See: Chapter 13

    5. According to this chapter, why does remote work sometimes create serious problems for people with ADHD, even though it also offers real benefits?¶

    1. Remote work removes the office's built-in structural scaffold — ambient body doubling, physical transitions, and visible social accountability — which must be deliberately rebuilt or the situation resembles the unstructured college cliff
    2. Remote work is illegal for employees with ADHD
    3. Remote work always improves ADHD symptoms with no downsides
    4. Remote work eliminates the need for any accommodations at all
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's honest two-sided view is that remote work removes the office's ambient structure — other people's presence, physical transitions, visible accountability — and without deliberately rebuilding that scaffold, the result resembles the unstructured college cliff. Option B is a fabricated legal claim. Option C ignores the losses the chapter details alongside the gains. Option D contradicts the chapter's point that structure must be actively rebuilt, not eliminated as a need.

    Concept Tested: Remote Work And ADHD

    See: Chapter 13

    6. What does this chapter identify as the central hazard of entrepreneurship for people with ADHD, despite its genuine appeal?¶

    1. Entrepreneurship is illegal for people with a disability
    2. Entrepreneurship never appeals to people with ADHD
    3. As their own manager, bookkeeper, and compliance department, a founder carries the entire admin tax personally with no institutional scaffolding, unless they deliberately buy or partner for those executive functions
    4. Founders with ADHD are required to disclose their diagnosis to investors
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter names the hazard plainly: a founder is their own manager, bookkeeper, and compliance department, so the entire admin tax lands unbuffered — the fix is deliberately buying or partnering for those executive functions early. Option A is a fabricated legal claim. Option B directly contradicts the chapter's statement that founders over-index on ADHD traits. Option D invents a disclosure requirement that doesn't exist.

    7. After a performance review that included one critical comment, an employee feels certain they are about to be fired and can't stop replaying the meeting. Using this chapter's approach to handling feedback at work, what should they do?¶

    1. Immediately quit before they can be fired
    2. Take notes during the meeting as a pause, then later sort the specific items into "fix" versus "feeling," treating the notes as information about work product rather than a verdict on their whole self
    3. Ignore all future feedback from that manager permanently
    4. Send an angry email to the manager right after the meeting to defend themselves
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's kit for handling feedback recommends note-taking as a built-in pause, then sorting items into "fix" versus "feeling" later, holding two readings rather than treating the feedback as a verdict on personhood. Option A is an extreme overreaction the chapter warns against. Option C would prevent legitimate course-correction the chapter values. Option D skips the pause the chapter specifically recommends before any written response.

    8. An employee has a large project due in six weeks and knows self-imposed personal deadlines rarely hold. Using this chapter's advice on deadlines at work, what should they do?¶

    1. Wait until the week before the deadline to start, since nothing else has worked before
    2. Send the manager a stage plan with specific interim dates, converting the distant deadline into several nearer ones with institutional visibility
    3. Ask the manager to remove the deadline altogether
    4. Keep the plan entirely private so no one notices if a stage slips
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter recommends manufacturing institutional witnesses by sending the manager a stage plan with dates, converting one distant deadline into several nearer ones with an audience — which often reads as exceptional professionalism. Option A repeats the pattern the scaffold is designed to prevent. Option C avoids the actual problem rather than solving it. Option D removes the audience effect that makes the stage plan work.

    9. Newly diagnosed and excited, someone plans to announce their ADHD to their entire team on Monday morning, believing "honesty is the right move." Based on this chapter's distinction between self-acceptance and disclosure, what is the most fitting advice?¶

    1. First identify what specific accommodation is actually needed, consider starting with an informal ask with no diagnosis attached, and treat a decision this permanent as worth writing down and holding for a week before acting on it
    2. Announce it immediately, since any hesitation means shame
    3. Never disclose ADHD at work under any circumstances, no matter the situation
    4. Disclosure decisions should always be made by a manager, not the employee
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's own worked example addresses this exact impulse: separate self-acceptance from strategic disclosure, identify the actual need, consider the informal graduated path first, and treat an irreversible decision made in diagnosis-week enthusiasm as worth writing down and holding before acting. Option B conflates hesitation with shame, which the chapter never claims. Option C forecloses a legitimate option the chapter treats as sometimes the right call. Option D wrongly removes the decision from the person it belongs to.

    10. A person notices they've said yes to every exciting new project this quarter, are masking exhaustion at the office daily, and have been running on deadline adrenaline for months — but they still feel fine because they aren't tired, just "busy." Using this chapter's burnout prevention framework, what is the most accurate analysis of their situation?¶

    1. Since they don't feel tired, there is no real risk of burnout
    2. Burnout can only happen to people without ADHD, since ADHD brains thrive on stimulation indefinitely
    3. This combination — overcommitment by enthusiasm, all-day masking, and adrenaline as a sustained operating system — is exactly the ADHD-specific burnout engine this chapter describes, and because burnout announces itself late, feeling fine now doesn't rule out an approaching collapse
    4. Working on exciting projects can never lead to burnout, regardless of quantity
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter names precisely this combination as the ADHD-specific burnout engine and warns that burnout announces itself late, which is why scheduled recovery and an outside early-warning observer matter more than how someone currently feels. Option A relies on the unreliable internal signal the chapter explicitly warns against. Option B contradicts Chapter 6's burnout mechanics, which apply directly to ADHD. Option D ignores the chapter's point that enthusiasm-driven overcommitment is exactly how burnout builds.
