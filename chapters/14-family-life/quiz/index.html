    Quiz: Family Life And Communication¶

    Test your understanding of how ADHD affects a family system, supporting an adult without parenting them, and communication that works with these review questions.

    1. According to this chapter, what is the central test that distinguishes support from parenting?¶

    1. Support is free; parenting always costs money
    2. Support happens in public; parenting happens in private
    3. Support is given by parents; parenting is given by anyone else
    4. Support is what the person asked for; parenting is what you decided they need
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's central test, worth printing and taping to a refrigerator, is that support is what the person asked for while parenting is what you decided they need — the same act, like setting up a calendar, can be either depending on who initiated it. Option A invents an unrelated financial distinction. Option B and C invent distinctions about location or family role that the chapter never makes.

    2. What is the mechanical difference between a reminder and a nag, according to this chapter?¶

    1. A reminder is requested, specific, and delivered through an agreed channel; a nag is unrequested, repeated, and delivered with rising emotional intensity
    2. A reminder is always written; a nag is always spoken aloud
    3. There is no real difference; both terms describe the same behavior
    4. A reminder comes from a parent; a nag comes from a partner
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter defines the difference mechanically rather than morally: a reminder is requested, specific, and delivered through an agreed channel, while a nag is unrequested, repeated, and rising in emotional charge. Option B invents a format-based distinction the chapter doesn't make. Option C denies a distinction the whole section is built around. Option D invents a relationship-based rule not in the text.

    Concept Tested: Reminders Without Nagging

    See: Chapter 14

    3. What four questions does this chapter suggest offering as a menu before providing help?¶

    1. Do you want money, time, space, or silence?
    2. Do you want praise, criticism, advice, or nothing?
    3. Do you want solutions, company, a reminder system, or just to vent?
    4. Do you want a doctor, a therapist, a coach, or a friend?
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's canonical ask-first menu is exactly this: solutions, company, a reminder system, or just to vent — offered explicitly rather than assumed. Options A, B, and D are plausible-sounding but invented menus not found in the chapter's text.

    4. According to this chapter's description of family system impact, what is the "compensator" role?¶

    1. A sibling who develops ADHD symptoms through observation
    2. A family member, usually a parent or partner, who absorbs the executive load — tracking appointments, forms, and refills — slowly converting from family member into logistics department
    3. A professional hired to manage the household's finances
    4. The family member who receives the ADHD diagnosis
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The compensator is the family member, usually a parent or partner, who absorbs the executive load and gradually converts from family member into logistics department. Option A describes an impossible transmission mechanism. Option C invents a professional role the chapter doesn't discuss in this context. Option D confuses the compensator with the person actually diagnosed.

    5. Why does this chapter recommend dividing household labor by "task shape" rather than a strict 50/50 split by volume?¶

    1. Because a 50/50 split is illegal in most households
    2. Because household labor should always fall entirely on the partner without ADHD
    3. Because task shape has no real effect on how reliably a task gets done
    4. Because matching tasks to what each partner's brain actually produces well — recurring calendar-shaped tasks versus burst, urgent tasks — reduces total household failure, as long as the fitted split is renegotiated as genuinely fair
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter recommends dividing by task shape — burst tasks versus recurring calendar-shaped tasks — because it reduces total household failure, provided the fitted split is honestly renegotiated as fair rather than defaulting to whoever "should" do more. Option A invents a legal claim. Option B contradicts the chapter's fairness requirement. Option C denies the entire mechanism the chapter describes.

    6. Why does this chapter insist that repair after conflict must include a system change, not just an apology or reconciliation?¶

    1. Because a repair that ends in reconciliation but no redesign leaves the same collision conditions in place, scheduling the same fight to recur
    2. Because apologies are meaningless and should never be offered
    3. Because system changes are required by family therapists in every case
    4. Because reconciliation always causes more conflict than it resolves
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's repair sequence ends with closing the loop through a system change, because a repair without redesign leaves the same triggering conditions in place for next month. Option B dismisses apologies, which the chapter treats as a necessary but insufficient step. Option C invents a professional requirement not stated. Option D contradicts the chapter's positive framing of reconciliation as part of repair.

    7. A father, worried about his adult daughter's incomplete registration renewal, wants to help. Based on this chapter's supporting-without-parenting test, which action counts as support rather than parenting?¶

    1. Installing a tracking app on her phone without telling her, "just to help"
    2. Mentioning the expired sticker once, then asking if she wants a hand, an alarm, or if it's already handled — and accepting her answer either way
    3. Renewing the registration himself without telling her
    4. Calling the DMV directly to handle it on her behalf
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. This matches the chapter's exact protocol: notice, offer once, ask which kind of help (or none) is wanted, and accept "it's handled" as a real answer. Option A is unrequested surveillance, explicitly named as parenting. Option C and D both act on her behalf without invitation, removing her ownership of the task and its consequences.

    8. A couple wants to discuss a difficult financial topic. Following this chapter's communication foundations, when and how should they have this conversation?¶

    1. At 9:30 pm, right when the topic comes up, however tired either person is
    2. In the middle of an unrelated argument, to save time
    3. During a calm, unhurried time such as a walk in daylight, rather than late at night on a drained executive battery
    4. Only through angry text messages sent in the heat of the moment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's communication foundations specifically recommend timing hard conversations for when the executive battery isn't drained — daylight, calm, ideally a walk — rather than late at night. Option A is the exact bad-timing example the chapter warns against. Option B stacks one conflict onto another rather than scheduling deliberately. Option D removes the calm, in-person conditions the chapter treats as essential.

    9. A mother whose 28-year-old son was recently diagnosed with ADHD notices he seems to have stopped taking his medication and hasn't replied to her last two texts asking about it. Based on this chapter's guidance on adult child autonomy, what is the most appropriate response?¶

    1. Offer once, without surveillance, to be a sounding board or specific kind of support if he ever wants it, then respect his answer — reserving direct intervention only for genuine safety concerns like self-harm
    2. Continue texting him daily about his medication until he responds
    3. Call his doctor directly to find out what's going on
    4. Show up unannounced at his home to confront him about the missed doses
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's own worked example addresses this nearly identical situation: treatment belongs to the adult child, unrequested monitoring is parenting rather than support, and the one exception to respecting autonomy is genuine safety concern, not a treatment choice. Option B continues the unrequested monitoring the chapter names as the actual problem. Option C and D both override his autonomy over his own treatment and privacy.

    10. A family notices that arguments about the same undone chore keep recurring — a demand arrives at a bad moment, gets ignored, is repeated with rising frustration, triggers a defensive reaction, ends in a blowup, and everyone comes away feeling their same old story was confirmed, only for tension to build again until the next demand. Using this chapter's analysis of the conflict cycle, what is the most effective way to break this pattern?¶

    1. The pattern can only be broken by the person with ADHD changing their behavior; the family member has no role in stopping it
    2. The only solution is to stop discussing the chore entirely and never bring it up again
    3. The cycle can be interrupted at any point by either person — by timing the demand better, moving the reminder to a system instead of a person, taking a pause during the defensive spike, or simply naming the loop out loud to reset the conversation
    4. Conflict cycles like this are permanent and cannot be interrupted once they begin
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's conflict cycle analysis identifies multiple exit points available to either person — timing, system-based reminders, the pause, or simply calling the loop out loud — none of which require waiting for the other person to act first. Option A wrongly assigns all responsibility to one person when the chapter names exits for both. Option B avoids the underlying issue rather than redesigning the interaction. Option D contradicts the chapter's entire point that the loop can be cut at any stage.
