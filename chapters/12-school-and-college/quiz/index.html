    Quiz: School And College¶

    Test your understanding of study strategies, school and college accommodations, and how to actually request support with these review questions.

    1. What is active recall?¶

    1. Rereading the same material multiple times until it feels familiar
    2. Listening to a lecture recording at double speed
    3. Closing the book and retrieving material from memory — flashcards, practice problems, brain dumps — rather than re-reading it
    4. Highlighting key terms in a textbook with colored markers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Active recall means closing the book and retrieving material from memory rather than re-reading it, which supplies instant feedback and exposes the illusion that familiarity equals knowledge. Option A describes the passive default the chapter argues against. Option B and D are other passive study habits the chapter doesn't identify as active recall.

    Concept Tested: Active Recall

    See: Chapter 12

    2. What is the key legal difference between an IEP and a 504 plan?¶

    1. A 504 plan provides accommodations that change conditions of access, while an IEP can also include specialized instruction, services, and measurable goals
    2. An IEP only applies to college students, while a 504 plan only applies to K-12 students
    3. A 504 plan requires a full team meeting and annual review, while an IEP requires no paperwork at all
    4. There is no real difference; the two terms describe the same legal document
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter explains a 504 plan changes conditions of access, while an IEP — under IDEA — can also include specialized instruction, services, and measurable annual goals with a heavier process. Option B reverses the actual scope; IEPs end at high school graduation, not college. Option C reverses which document has the heavier process. Option D denies a distinction the chapter treats as central.

    Concept Tested: IEP

    See: Chapter 12

    3. According to this chapter, what is the key structural difference between K-12 accommodations and college disability services?¶

    1. College accommodations are automatically identical to whatever plan existed in high school
    2. Colleges automatically contact parents whenever a student's grades decline
    3. IEPs continue to apply automatically once a student enters college
    4. In college, the student must self-identify, provide documentation, and activate accommodations each term — parents are legally outside the process
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's central structural point is that college disability services activate only when the student self-identifies and provides documentation, with parents legally outside the process under FERPA. Option A is false; college accommodations require new registration, not automatic carryover. Option B contradicts FERPA privacy protections the chapter describes. Option C is directly contradicted; IDEA and IEPs end at high school graduation.

    Concept Tested: College Disability Services

    See: Chapter 12

    4. Why does this chapter describe school accommodations as "the ramp, not a head start"?¶

    1. Because accommodations give students an unfair advantage over their peers
    2. Because accommodations are only available to students with perfect grades
    3. Because accommodations remove the disability's impact from the measurement, so the material being tested — not the time blindness or working memory limits — is what actually gets assessed
    4. Because accommodations replace the need to learn the material at all
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's ramp analogy means accommodations remove the disability's impact from what's being measured, so a chemistry exam tests chemistry rather than time blindness or working memory. Option A treats a leveling measure as an unfair edge, which the chapter explicitly rejects. Option B invents a grade requirement that doesn't exist. Option D wildly overstates what accommodations change.

    Concept Tested: School Accommodations

    See: Chapter 12

    5. Why does this chapter recommend using an app that automatically schedules spaced repetition reviews, rather than relying on remembering to review?¶

    1. Because running a review schedule on prospective memory invites the same unreliable-alarm failure this book has described throughout, so a tool that fires on its own is more dependable
    2. Because apps are required by every school's official curriculum
    3. Because spaced repetition doesn't actually improve retention
    4. Because apps guarantee perfect exam scores regardless of study quality
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter is explicit that spaced repetition shouldn't run on prospective memory, since that's an unreliable system, and recommends tools that fire automatically instead. Option B invents a curriculum requirement. Option C contradicts the chapter's endorsement of spaced repetition as high-value. Option D overstates the guarantee any study tool can offer.

    Concept Tested: Spaced Repetition

    See: Chapter 12

    6. Why does this chapter treat genuine interest as a "structural requirement, not a luxury" when choosing a college major?¶

    1. Because interest has no real effect on academic performance
    2. Because a major that doesn't hold genuine interest will extract a heavy procrastination tax every week, while one that grabs the student can draw on hyperfocus for free
    3. Because only students without ADHD need to consider interest when choosing a major
    4. Because interest guarantees a major will lead to a high-paying career
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter argues that the interest-based nervous system makes genuine interest structural: a boring major extracts a weekly procrastination tax, while an interesting one draws on hyperfocus for free. Option A contradicts the chapter's central point about interest and performance. Option C is backwards; interest matters especially for ADHD brains. Option D makes a career-outcome claim the chapter never states.

    Concept Tested: Choosing A Major

    See: Chapter 12

    7. A parent wants to request accommodations for their child and is deciding between simply mentioning the diagnosis to the teacher at conferences or sending a formal request. Based on this chapter's playbook, which approach is more likely to succeed?¶

    1. Mentioning it casually at conferences, since teachers will remember and act on verbal requests
    2. Waiting until grades drop significantly before making any request
    3. Asking the student to explain their own needs to the teacher without any parental involvement
    4. Putting the request in writing, attaching the evaluation report, and listing specific, mechanism-linked accommodations rather than a general request for help
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's Amara example contrasts a casual mention that changes nothing with a written request, attached documentation, and specific asks that produce a real, signed plan. Option A is exactly the ineffective approach the chapter's worked example shows failing. Option B delays past the point where the process should start. Option C skips the parent's role in elementary and middle school, where the chapter still expects parental involvement.

    Concept Tested: Requesting Accommodations

    See: Chapter 12

    8. A college student struggles with time blindness during long exams. Which test-taking strategy from this chapter directly addresses this problem?¶

    1. Skipping the exam entirely to avoid the anxiety
    2. Writing a per-section time budget on the exam margin at the start and setting checkpoint questions to track pacing
    3. Answering questions in a random order with no plan
    4. Relying on the professor to announce when time is running low
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter specifically recommends writing a time budget in the margin at minute zero and setting checkpoint questions to counter time blindness during a fixed-time exam. Option A avoids the problem rather than addressing it. Option C removes the triage strategy the chapter recommends. Option D outsources time management to someone who isn't obligated to provide it.

    Concept Tested: Test-Taking Strategies

    See: Chapter 12

    9. A parent, worried about their college freshman's collapsing grades, calls the dean's office and the student's professors directly to request accommodations on the student's behalf. Based on this chapter's explanation of college disability services, what is the accurate response to this situation?¶

    1. This isn't discrimination — post-secondary law makes the student the sole driver of the accommodation process, and FERPA bars the college from discussing the student's situation with a parent regardless of who pays
    2. The college is required to work with the parent instead of the student, since the parent is paying tuition
    3. The professors are legally required to grant accommodations immediately upon a parent's request
    4. This approach is exactly what the chapter recommends parents do to help
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's own worked example addresses this exact situation: it isn't discrimination, since post-secondary law makes the student the accommodation process's sole driver and FERPA bars the school from discussing the student's situation with a parent. Option B misunderstands FERPA and who tuition payment entitles to information. Option C invents an obligation professors don't have toward a parent's request. Option D contradicts the chapter's explicit guidance that parental help must be indirect at this stage.

    Concept Tested: College Accommodations

    See: Chapter 12

    10. A school tells a family "she's passing all her classes, so she doesn't need a 504 plan," even though the family suspects she is masking significant struggle. Applying this chapter's reasoning together with Chapter 4's concept of high-achievement masking, what is the strongest analysis of the school's position?¶

    1. The school is correct; passing grades are the only valid standard for whether accommodations are needed
    2. Since she is passing, pursuing a 504 plan would be dishonest and should be dropped entirely
    3. Passing grades can coexist with high private cost from masking, so the disability's impact on access — not the grade outcome — is the legal standard, and the family should ask directly what the grades are costing her
    4. Masking only affects children who are failing, so it doesn't apply to a student who is passing
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter directly names this failure mode and its counter: passing is not the legal standard, impact on access is, and Chapter 4's masking concept is the evidence that a passing grade can hide serious private cost worth asking about explicitly. Option A accepts the very standard the chapter says is wrong. Option B mistakes advocating for real needs as dishonesty. Option D contradicts the entire premise of high-achievement masking, which specifically describes struggle hidden behind good grades.

    Concept Tested: School Accommodations
